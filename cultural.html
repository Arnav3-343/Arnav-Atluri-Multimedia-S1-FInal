Cultural Art Unit

A collection of cultural artwork, reflections, and identity pieces.

Introduction

The Cultural Art Unit allowed me to explore the deeper meaning behind my cultural identity and the stories that shaped me. Through each artwork, I reflected on traditions, values, and memories that connect me to my heritage. This project helped me understand how culture influences my creativity and the way I see the world. By combining symbolism, research, and personal experiences, I created pieces that represent who I am and where I come from. This unit allowed me to express my identity in a meaningful and artistic way.

Reflection

Throughout this unit, I learned how powerful it is to express culture through art and storytelling. Creating each piece required me to think about the values, memories, and traditions that define my identity. I reflected on how my cultural background influences my perspective and the way I connect with the world around me. This project helped me appreciate the importance of preserving cultural stories and sharing them with others. Overall, the Cultural Art Unit strengthened my understanding of myself and allowed me to express my heritage in a creative and personal way.

Redesigned Flags & Explanation

The flag I made on the right represents the god Krishna's fight with the serpent Kaliya. Kaliya had been polluting the Yamuna River with his deadly poison; this resulted in the death of much nearby aquatic life as well as unsafe drinking conditions for people. The reason I chose this specific scene in Indian mythology is because it recounts one of the most memorable encounters with one of my favorite gods, Krishna, and how passionate he was about all life. I chose dark blue to represent the serpent because that was how it was depicted in several iterations of the story, as well as the red eyes for dramatic emphasis. Krishna was in a light blue healing circle demonstrating how he fought the demon and healed the river of all its deadly poison. The flag I created on the left represents the meaning of my name, Arnav, which means “the primordial ocean.” I chose to represent it with waves in front of the Taj Mahal because that is one of the first places I remember visiting with my grandparents in India.
